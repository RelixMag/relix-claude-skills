## Dark Mode FAQ for Publishers

**URL:** https://docs.joinlede.com/site-configuration-wordpress-admin/dark-mode-faq-for-publishers/

**Contents:**
- Dark Mode FAQ for Publishers
- How do I change my dark mode compatible logo?
- How do I change my dark mode compatible brand colors?
- Can I opt out of dark mode for my site on the Lede platform?
- How do I preview content in dark mode?

A dark mode-compatible version of your logo is required to ensure sufficient contrast on a dark background. You can upload this logo directly to your site by navigating to: WordPress Admin > Appearance > Customize > Site Identity > Select image for “Dark Theme logo” > Publish

Dark mode-compatible brand colors are required to ensure sufficient contrast on a dark background. You can set your dark mode primary color in the Publisher App under Settings > Appearance > Colors > Dark Theme Primary Color.

In addition, please consider what you want for your dark mode-compatible secondary brand color, which is primarily used as the highlight color. You can set it in the Publisher App under Settings > Appearance > Colors > Dark Theme Secondary Color.

You can disable the public-facing dark mode toggle for your site by turning off “Dark theme option enabled” in the Publisher App under Settings > Appearance. When this is off, readers will not see a light/dark switch in the header or in article previews. Dark mode is enabled by default for new Lede sites.

To preview content in dark mode, click to Preview as you normally would and then toggle on dark mode via the light switch.

## Guide to Subcategory Navigation

**URL:** https://docs.joinlede.com/site-configuration-wordpress-admin/guide-to-subcategory-navigation/

**Contents:**
- Guide to Subcategory Navigation
- User Experience benefits
- Business benefits
- How it appears
  - 5 or fewer subcategories.
  - More than 5 subcategories.
- How to activate subcategory navigation

Subcategory navigation is a new feature that provides enhanced visibility for subcategories, making category hierarchies clear and showcasing the breadth and depth of available subcategories. This feature may deliver valuable user experience and business benefits.

Improved Navigation Efficiency: Users can quickly scan and jump to their specific area of interest without navigating through multiple pages or relying on search functionality.

Reduced Cognitive Load: Clear subcategory organization helps users mentally map the site structure and understand available content. This reduces decision paralysis and makes browsing more intuitive.

Enhanced Discoverability: Users often discover related interests they weren’t initially seeking, leading to serendipitous content discovery.

Faster Task Completion: Whether users are browsing casually or pursuing specific goals, visible subcategories create more direct pathways to relevant content, reducing the number of clicks and page loads required.

Increased Page Views and Session Duration: By making it easier for users to explore related subcategories, you naturally encourage deeper site engagement and longer browsing sessions.

Better SEO Performance: Subcategory links create clear internal linking structures that help search engines understand your site taxonomy. Each subcategory link also provides keyword-rich anchor text that can boost rankings for those terms.

Improved Conversion Opportunities: More efficient navigation means users are more likely to find content they want to engage with, reducing bounce rates and increasing conversion potential.

Enhanced Analytics and User Insights: Clear category separation makes it easier to track which content types are most popular, informing content strategy and business decisions.

The subcategory navigation displays on the parent category page, with subcategories listed in alphabetical order.

When there are 5 or fewer subcategories, they display in a sidebar menu to the left of the river or grid of posts. In the example shown, Gentrification, Investigate Report, and Protests are subcategories of News.

When there are more than 5 subcategories, they display in a three-across grid layout, replacing the river of posts. Category featured images are optional in this view. In the example shown, Architecture, Books, Comix, Fiction, Film, and Signage are subcategories of Art.

In this view, category featured images are used and the number of articles in each subcategory displays under the subcategory title.

⚠️ Important Note: Subcategory navigation does not display when there are more than 24 subcategories.

Subcategory navigation is optional and turned off by default.

## Article River Settings

**URL:** https://docs.joinlede.com/site-configuration-wordpress-admin/article-river-settings/

**Contents:**
- Article River Settings
- The Settings
  - Article River Divider Color
  - Article River Content
  - Article River Layout
- Other settings that apply to the Article River
  - Lede Lite

Article River Settings allow publishers to customize the content and appearance of the recirculation module at the bottom of individual articles known as the Article River. The Article River contains 6 articles.

The Article River settings are global and apply to all articles on the site. There are not currently any settings that allow the global Article River settings to be overridden on a per-article or per-category basis.

The Article River on PopHeist, which uses a custom Article River Divider Color

The settings for the Article River can be found in the Publisher App under Settings > Appearance, in the “Posts & Pages” group.

Set the color of the border between article river cards. The default color is light grey. You can also use your primary, secondary, or accent color. In dark mode, the border is light grey.

This setting determines which posts display. There are two options: default and category. Both options remove the current article from the list.

The default setting displays the most recent articles chronologically. The heading for the Article River with the default setting is “More from [Publication Name].” The CTA is “See all posts” and it links to the /all page.

The category setting displays the most recent articles from the post’s primary category. If there is no primary category set for an article, the most recent posts will display. The heading for the Article River with the category setting is “More from [Category Name].” The CTA is “Explore [Category Name]” and it links to the category’s archive page at /category/category-name. Example with the category Movies. The heading will be “More from Movies” and the CTA will be “Explore Movies,” which will link to the /category/movies.

The category content will only display if the primary category has more than 3 or more posts and if the primary category is not “Uncategorized.” In those cases, the chronological posts will display for that post as a backup.

This setting determines the Article River layout. There are two options: default and three across.

The default setting displays a vertical river of posts. The CTA is at the bottom of the river.

The three across setting displays a three across grid of posts on desktop screens. On medium sized screens, the grid is two across, and the posts stack on mobile. The CTA is at the top of the river.

The Sponsorship River Card will not display in three across layout. This is similar to other instances of the three across layout.

The Article River on Lede FYI, which uses the “three across” Article River Layout and the “category” Article River Content

The “Archive & Single Article Post Rivers” Lede Lite setting also applies to the Article River. When enabled, images do not display in the Article River regardless of the content or layout selections above. This toggle is not currently exposed in the Publisher App — please contact Lede support if you would like it enabled or disabled for your site.
